The gap we close

By 2030, four in ten of the world’s young people will be African. By 2040, Africa is projected to have the world’s largest working-age population. That is a generation of people building businesses, creating products, shaping culture and solving problems at scale.

The building is happening. The question is whether the value being created is being properly recognised, protected and rewarded.

A business can have paying customers, strong margins and a product people genuinely need, yet struggle to secure the right partnership or funding. Its pitch may fail to communicate the strength of its model. Its commercial terms may leave too much exposed. Its brand may borrow language that makes it harder to distinguish what it does exceptionally well. Its grant proposal may tell a familiar story while missing the evidence that makes this particular venture worth backing.

And these weaknesses do not only surface in capital rooms. They show up in pricing decisions, supplier agreements, distribution deals, daily communications and opportunities that become harder to negotiate when the underlying structure is not sound.

What is built on the ground should have the structure to stand up in the room, the leverage to negotiate on fairer terms, and the foundations to keep growing beyond its founder’s immediate reach. Build the value. Make it legible. Protect what it can become.

That is the gap BWAFA exists to close: the distance between what African builders have created and the value they can credibly communicate, commercially protect and carry forward.

The Firm

A paid practice. Built for the heavy labour.

The Firm is a paid practice. It is a dedicated team of specialists putting in the heavy labour behind closed doors to ensure your venture is built to last, investment-ready, and audit-ready. We do not do free deck reviews, quick favours, or casual strategy calls. If we are digging into your numbers, contracts, or pitch decks, it is a paid engagement, every single time.

We charge for our expertise because high-level structural work requires real labour, and feeding the brain trust behind that work is how we keep building across Africa.

What we do

The Firm brings commercial thinking, strategic positioning and legal intelligence to the work of building stronger ventures. Six specialist lanes, shaped around what your business needs to move forward.

01

Brand Narrative & Pitch Clarity

We turn what your company actually does, why it matters and what sets it apart into a clear, credible narrative that holds up under scrutiny.

02

Commercial Architecture

We examine how your business makes money, where margins are under pressure, how your pricing holds up and whether the model supports the growth you’re pursuing.

03

Communication Audits

From your pitch deck to your DMs, we examine how your business communicates across the places that shape perception, identifying what strengthens your credibility, what weakens it and what needs to change.

04

Grant, Pitch & Investor Narrative

Not just pitch decks, though those get fixed too. We develop funding applications and proposals around your actual strengths, supporting evidence and intended impact, giving reviewers a clear reason to take your venture seriously.

05

Legal Intelligence Applied

We review, draft and pressure-test contractual terms to help identify overlooked risks, protect your interests and strengthen your position before the stakes get higher.

06

EdTech & Learning Design

We design learning experiences, curricula and educational platforms around how people actually learn, participate and progress, not simply how much content they can consume.

The outcome: A business whose value is easier to understand, whose positioning competitors can’t easily copy and whose commercial foundations are better equipped to support its next stage. Work built around what is actually true about your business. No borrowed voice. Period.

What we don't do

We review, draft and restructure. We do not act as your registered legal counsel or file legal documents on your behalf. Where a matter requires formal legal representation or jurisdiction-specific legal services, a licensed lawyer must handle that work.

We can help identify the right legal expertise for your situation & recommend an appropriate firm in your country, where possible.

Our role is to help you understand the issues, strengthen the work within our scope and approach the right legal professional better prepared. Because the terms you sign can shape what you own, what you owe and what you can do next.

The mind behind BWAFA, in service of the institution

This isn't a personality spotlight. It's context for how BWAFA thinks, decides, and advocates for African ventures.

Long before BWAFA had a name, Our Founder was the person who noticed when something didn't add up: a conversation tilted against the quieter voice, a process that rewarded presentation over substance, a decision that treated African builders as risk before it recognised their rigour. The instinct was never theatrical. It was practical, persistent, and hard to switch off, the kind of thing that got her called "firm and instructive" by a courtroom panel before she'd even finished the degree.

Over time, that attention moved from individual moments to a pattern worth naming. Strong ventures kept struggling to be legible to capital. Founder stories kept getting forced into borrowed language. Contracts and commercial structures kept arriving late, once the leverage was already gone. BWAFA was built in response to that pattern, not as commentary from the sidelines, but as operating work done alongside founders.

Cultural authority, paired with commercial precision. That's the whole method.

The founder story matters only because it explains that method: clearer decks, sharper investor narratives, revenue architecture that holds, and legal intelligence applied early enough to protect ownership while growth is still being designed, not after the leverage is gone.
